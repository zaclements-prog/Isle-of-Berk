# Handoff: physics-based flight controller (RL training pipeline)

**Audience.** An engineer or coding agent working locally on an Apple Silicon Mac (M5 Max). This document is meant to
be self-contained.

**Status.** This is a separate track from the main build. The authored Blender animation pipeline remains the source
of truth for the game. This track explores whether a physically simulated, learned flight controller can produce wing
motion that genuinely works the air. That is the approach of Won et al., "How to Train Your Dragon: Example-Guided
Control of Flapping Flight" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2017) and its follow-up "Aerobatics Control of Flying Creatures via
Self-Regulated Learning" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2018).

**Ground truth for style.** The authored clips described below are the look to match. In side-by-side review, the
authored animation beat a hand-tuned physics chain or matched it. Physics alone is not the goal. The goal is authored
style plus physical plausibility.

---

## 1. Goal and deliverables

Train controllers for a physically simulated Toothless that perform these skills while imitating the authored
reference clips:

| # | Skill | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | takeoff | From standing: crouch, unfold, push off, climb on heavy wingbeats. |
| 2 | cruise flap | Steady level flight at about 12 m/s, about 1.1 Hz beats. |
| 3 | glide | Wings spread, slow descent or hold. |
| 4 | bank / turn | Steerable heading, ±60°/s. Added on top of 2 and 3. |
| 5 | landing | Flare, then touch down on the hind legs and then the fore legs. Stretch goal. |

**Deliverables**, each under `ml/flight/` in the repo (create it):

1. **`model/toothless.xml`.** A MuJoCo MJCF physics model of Toothless, generated by a script from the rig (§3), not
   written by hand.
2. **`refs/*.npz`.** The reference trajectories exported from the authored clips (§4).
3. **`train/`.** The environment, reward and PPO training code (§5–6), plus configs and a `README.md` with exact
   commands.
4. **`out/policies/<skill>.onnx`.** Trained policies, with their observation and action normalisation stats as JSON.
5. **`out/rollouts/<skill>.npz`.** Recorded rollouts of each trained policy: per-frame bone rotations at 30 fps in the
   same bone set and convention as the references. This is the **primary hand-back to the game** (§8).
6. **`out/report.md`.** For each skill: training curves, imitation error per body part, and task metrics (altitude
   held, speed, heading error, energy). Include rendered videos or GIFs of the rollouts in a side view and a
   three-quarter view.

Do **not** modify the game engine (`src/`) or the authored Blender pipeline outside `ml/flight/`, except for the
small read-only export script in §4.

## 3. Physics model (MJCF), generated from the rig

Write `ml/flight/tools/make_mjcf.py`. Plain Python is fine: import `anatomy.py` directly, since it only needs numpy.

- **Bodies.** One rigid body per bone in this set:
  - pelvis;
  - spine_01–03 and chest;
  - neck_01–04 and head;
  - tail_01–12;
  - per side: scapula, humerus, radius, metacarpal (with the toes merged); femur, tibia, metatarsal (with the toes
    merged); wing_humerus and wing_forearm;
  - per side, the hand: either one rigid **hand plate**, or 7 rib bodies (start rigid).
- **Geometry.** Use capsules along each bone, with radii from `TAIL_RADII` and the proxy spheres. Model the wing
  membrane as thin boxes or plates spanning between the forearm, the ribs and the body attach line
  (`MAIN_WING_L["attach"]`). It needs mass and area, but it is **not** a cloth simulation.
- **Mass.** Choose a total mass and distribute it by approximate volume. The trunk carries about 55% and the tail
  about 12%. The wing bones and membrane are light, about 4% per wing. The total is a tuning choice: start around
  **300 kg**. Higher means a bigger lift requirement.
- **Joints.**
  - Spine, neck and tail: ball joints, or three hinges, limited by `CHAIN_LIMITS`.
  - Legs: hinges limited by `LIMBS`. The hip, shoulder and scapula can be 2-DoF.
  - Wing shoulder: 3-DoF (elevation, sweep, pronation). Wing elbow: 1–2 DoF (the fold). Wrist and fan: 1 DoF (the
    fan spread) if you model ribs. Wing ranges come from the authored stroke extremes in `flight.py` `TAKEOFF` /
    `flight_loops.FLAP`, plus a margin.
- **Actuation.** PD position servos (MuJoCo `<position>` actuators) per DoF. The policy outputs target angles (or
  offsets from the reference pose). Pick gains so that tracking the reference without aerodynamics is stable.

### Aerodynamics

The wings must produce lift. You have two options:

- **(A) Quasi-steady panels.** This is what the papers use. Split each wing into about 8–20 flat panels. Each step,
  for each panel, compute:
  - the local air velocity, `v = −(body velocity at the panel centroid)`, plus wind if any;
  - the angle of attack α against the panel normal;
  - lift `L = ½ ρ C_L(α) A |v|²`, perpendicular to v;
  - drag `D = ½ ρ C_D(α) A |v|²`, along v.

  Use flat-plate coefficients: `C_L = 2 sin α cos α` (or a fitted curve with stall around 15–20°) and
  `C_D = 2 sin² α + C_D0`, with ρ = 1.225. Apply the force at the panel centroid through `data.xfrc_applied`. Add the
  same drag model, at a lower coefficient, to the trunk and tail.
- **(B) MuJoCo's built-in ellipsoid fluid model** (`<option density="1.225" viscosity=".."/>` plus
  `fluidshape="ellipsoid"` on geoms). It is quick to try, but it is a rougher fit for flapping membranes.
  Prototype with B, and switch to A if the lift is unconvincing.

**Sanity checks before any RL:**
- Play the **reference wing stroke kinematically** (driving the wing joints, with the body free) and measure the
  vertical force per beat. It should be in the right ballpark to hold up the chosen mass at about 1.1 Hz. If it isn't,
  tune the panel areas and mass first.
- The glide should give a lift-to-drag ratio of roughly 3–8.

Simulate at **480–1000 Hz physics** with the policy at **30 Hz**. Wing tips move fast, so use small steps.

## 4. Reference motions (export from the authored clips)

Add `pipeline/blender/toothless/export_refs.py`. It is read-only with respect to the pipeline and runs in Blender. For
each clip (`flight.takeoff_frames()`, `flight_loops.flap_frames()`, `flight_loops.glide_frames()`), pose every frame
exactly as the previews do (`flight.apply_frame` / `flight_loops.apply_loop_frame`) and record:

- **root:** the pelvis world position and orientation (include the forward travel `x`, and the height from `root` and
  the body line);
- **joints:** per bone, the local rotation as a quaternion (`pose_bone.matrix_basis`) for every bone in the §3 set,
  plus the wing bones' armature-space directions (handy for the panel targets);
- **contacts:** paw contact flags (takeoff: all four planted until the push-off frame);
- **meta:** fps = 30, loop = true / false, period, forward speed.

Save one file per clip as `ml/flight/refs/<clip>.npz` (with a JSON sidecar listing the bone order). The bone axes must
match the MJCF joints. Write a test that maps a reference frame onto the MJCF model and checks that the joint
positions agree within 1 cm.

## 5. Learning setup (DeepMimic-style imitation plus task)

- **Algorithm.** PPO (actor-critic), Gaussian policy, GAE, normalised observations.
  - Use `stable-baselines3` or `rsl_rl`, or a small custom PyTorch PPO. Custom gives the most control.
  - The papers used a hierarchical deep RL scheme plus evolution strategies (2017) and self-regulated learning
    (2018). Start with plain PPO plus imitation, which is the modern default, and add their curriculum ideas if
    learning stalls (§7).
- **Policy and value networks.** MLPs, 2–3 hidden layers of 512–1024 units (start at 1024-512), with ELU or tanh.
  That is roughly 0.5–1.5 M parameters, a few MB.
- **Observation:**
  - root height, orientation (6D), linear and angular velocity in the body frame;
  - per joint, angle and velocity;
  - wing panel airspeeds and angles of attack (optional, helpful);
  - reference phase (sin/cos);
  - task command (target speed, heading rate, climb rate).
- **Action.** PD targets as an offset from the reference pose at the current phase, scaled to about ±0.5 rad.
- **Reward:** `r = w_p r_pose + w_v r_vel + w_e r_end + w_r r_root + w_t r_task − w_u r_effort`.
  - Use exponentiated errors as in DeepMimic: pose (joint quaternions), velocity, end effectors (wing tips, paws,
    head, tail tip), root height and orientation, and the task (speed, heading, altitude-rate tracking).
  - Start with weights of about 0.5 / 0.05 / 0.15 / 0.1 / 0.2 and an effort penalty of about 0.01.
- **Episode tools:**
  - **Reference state initialisation:** start episodes at random phases of the clip.
  - **Early termination:** end the episode if the head or trunk touches the ground in flight, if the orientation
    deviates more than about 60° from the reference, or if the altitude error exceeds a threshold.
  - **Domain randomisation, later:** mass ±10%, wind gusts, panel coefficients ±15%.
- **Curriculum.** Train cruise flap first (a loop, easiest), then glide, then takeoff (contacts plus transition), then
  turn/bank (a command input), then landing.

## 6. Compute plan on the M5 Max

- **Simulation.** MuJoCo on the CPU cores (`pip install mujoco`), many environments in parallel with
  `gymnasium.vector.AsyncVectorEnv` or `multiprocessing`. Use one environment per performance core.
  - GPU-parallel simulators (MuJoCo MJX via JAX, Isaac Lab) mostly target NVIDIA GPUs. Treat JAX's Metal backend as
    experimental: try MJX only if it works reliably on the Mac.
- **Networks.** PyTorch on the GPU (`device="mps"`) or the CPU. These MLPs are small, so CPU is often comparable.
  Benchmark both.
- **Expected scale.** These are estimates, to verify. Expect tens of millions of environment steps per skill. At
  about 20–60 k sim-steps per second aggregate on CPU, that is **hours to about 2 days per skill**.
- **Logging.** Log to TensorBoard or W&B, checkpoint every N updates, and keep the best policy by evaluation return.
- **Fallback.** If iteration is too slow, rent an NVIDIA GPU and use MJX or Isaac Lab for the heavy runs. Keep the
  code simulator-agnostic where it is cheap to do so.

## 7. Milestones and acceptance

| M | Done when |
|---|-----------|
| M0 | Blender builds locally; `export_refs.py` writes all three refs; the ref-to-MJCF mapping test passes. |
| M1 | The MJCF model loads. Kinematic playback of the reference stroke produces lift within 30% of body weight per beat (cruise). The aero unit tests pass (flat plate at known α gives the expected L and D). |
| M2 | **Cruise flap**: holds altitude ±0.5 m and speed ±1 m/s for 30 s without terminating. Pose imitation error under about 10° RMS on the body and under about 15° on the wings. |
| M3 | **Glide**: holds for 10 s, with a sink rate and attitude that look plausible. Smooth transitions flap ↔ glide on command. |
| M4 | **Takeoff** from the standing pose to stable cruise within about 4 s, matching the authored takeoff's beats. |
| M5 | **Turn/bank** following a heading-rate command ±60°/s with a coordinated bank. |
| M6 | Optional: **landing**. |

At each milestone, write rollouts and videos to `out/` and update `out/report.md`. **Compare every rollout side by
side with the authored clip**: style fidelity is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.

## 8. Hand-back to the game (contract)

**Primary: baked rollouts.** `out/rollouts/<skill>.npz` holds per-frame local bone quaternions at 30 fps for the §3 bone set,
with the same names and axes as `refs/`, plus the root trajectory. The main pipeline then bakes these into
`toothless.poses.glb` with `library.keyframe_frames`, exactly like the authored clips. **No physics needs to run in
the browser** for this path. The fore-arm springs and the head leveller from `flight.py` can still be layered on top.

**Secondary: live control.** This is an option for later, so don't build it now.
- Export the policy as ONNX, with the normalisation stats.
- Runtime would need the MJCF dynamics in the browser, through a MuJoCo WebAssembly build or a port to Rapier, plus
  onnxruntime-web.
- Note any blockers you find, but don't build it.

Keep these stable for the game side: the bone names, quaternion convention (w, x, y, z), the armature frame (+Y behind,
+Z up, metres) and the fps.

## 9. Things to verify first (unknowns)

- **The papers' details.** Read both papers: the wing aerodynamics model, network sizes, training time and hardware.
  The PDFs are at `mrl.snu.ac.kr/research/ProjectDragon/` and `mrl.snu.ac.kr/research/ProjectAerobatics/`, and in the
  ACM Digital Library (DOI 10.1145/3130800.3130833 and 10.1145/3272127.3275023).
  - The 2018 paper states a training duration that we haven't read: the snippet we saw was truncated at "The learning
    process took 3 to…".
  - Adjust §5–6 to what they report.
- **MuJoCo on Apple Silicon.** CPU performance for this model size, at about 60 bodies with panels.
- **The panel model.** Whether it yields enough lift at a plausible mass and flapping rate. If not, scale the membrane
  area or relax the mass. Flight in this game is stylised, not a biology simulation.
